Motivation: To demonstrate experimentally that indefinite causal order—where the order of operations is placed in quantum superposition—can be exploited as a resource for quantum cryptography, specifically to detect eavesdropping without sacrificing key material, which standard BB84 protocols cannot achieve since they require public disclosure and discard of a fraction of the raw key.

Method: A BB84-like quantum cryptographic protocol is implemented by placing Alice and Bob's measurement-and-preparation operations in a photonic quantum SWITCH. Eavesdropping is detected via measurements of the control qubit rather than by key comparison. A new measurement technique enables polarization measurement of a photon inside the quantum SWITCH without destroying path coherence. The protocol retains every qubit for key generation while simultaneously testing for eavesdroppers.

Result: The experiment achieves an average eavesdropper detection probability of 0.15 ± 0.02 per shared qubit. Eavesdropping detection is performed through control-qubit measurements. However, the current implementation does not yet constitute a secure QKD protocol due to the post-selection required for measurements within the quantum SWITCH.

Conclusion: The work provides a proof of principle that indefinite causal order can be exploited to detect eavesdropping in quantum cryptography without sacrificing key bits. While not yet a fully secure QKD protocol, it opens the door for future protocols that leverage quantum SWITCH structures to enhance quantum key distribution efficiency.

Abstract: In quantum physics the order in which different operations occur can be placed in superposition. The resulting processes have an indefinite causal order and are both of fundamental interest and can be viewed as a novel quantum resource that enables a variety of new protocols. Here we report an experimental implementation of one such protocol, where we perform BB84-like quantum cryptography by placing Alice and Bob's measurement-and-preparation operations in a photonic quantum SWITCH. By embedding Alice and Bob within the quantum SWITCH, the protocol achieves an average eavesdropper detection probability of $0.15 \pm 0.02$ per shared qubit, with eavesdropper detection performed through measurements of the control qubit rather than by comparing the key. Unlike the standard BB84 and related schemes, which detect eavesdropping by publicly revealing and discarding a fraction of the raw key, our approach requires no disclosure of key material: every retained qubit can, in principle, be tested for eavesdropping while remaining available for key generation. The experiment relies on a new measurement technique that allows the polarization of a photon to be measured inside the quantum SWITCH without destroying path coherence. Although the present implementation does not yet constitute a secure quantum key distribution protocol, owing to the post-selection required for measurements within the quantum SWITCH, it provides a proof of principle that indefinite causal order can be exploited to detect eavesdropping without sacrificing key bits.
