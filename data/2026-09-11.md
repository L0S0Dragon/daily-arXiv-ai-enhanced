A candidate construction in the surviving regime is proposed under a new, clearly stated security assumption.

The explicit reversible quantum circuit for block inversion is validated in a register-level simulator. The exact structural boundary where the method stops is identified, and a candidate construction in the surviving regime is proposed. Results are supported by reproducible software artifacts and experiments.

Conclusion: The hardness of the dihedral HSP does not protect group-ring-based public-key schemes, because unit inversion is a distinct problem solvable via block decomposition without an HSP solver. Main category: cond-mat.dis-nn

TL;DR: The paper shows that the arithmetic class of an irrational frequency, quantified by its irrationality measure, directly controls the infrared scaling of long-wavelength charge fluctuations in translation-covariant quasiperiodic systems.

Motivation: Quasiperiodic order is characterized by irrational frequencies, and rational approximability (irrationality measure) defines distinct arithmetic classes. The authors aim to establish that this arithmetic classification has direct physical consequences for long-wavelength charge fluctuations.

Method: They analyze translation-covariant quasiperiodic systems, relating the infrared scaling of charge fluctuation to the interplay between the irrationality exponent of the irrational frequency and the large-harmonic decay of the hull charge profile. They also prove a statement about gapped occupied states and extend the framework to general translation-covariant multi-frequency quasiperiodic systems.

Result: The infrared scaling is governed by two factors: the large-harmonic decay of the hull charge profile determines the available charge weight, while the irrationality exponent controls how efficiently that weight is transferred to the infrared. All algebraic irrational frequencies share the same arithmetic scaling, whereas exceptionally well-approximable transcendental frequencies can exhibit strongly enhanced infrared fluctuation scaling. Occupied states separated from the Fermi level by a gap stable throughout the hull contribute only an analytic infrared background, leaving the nontrivial scaling to near-Fermi states.

Conclusion: Arithmetic classification of irrational frequencies has direct physical consequences: algebraic irrationals exhibit universal scaling, while exceptionally well-approximable transcendentals show enhanced infrared fluctuation scaling. Only near-Fermi states contribute the nontrivial scaling, and the results generalize to multi-frequency quasiperiodic systems.

Abstract: Quasiperiodic order is characterized by irrational frequencies whose rational approximability known as irrationality measure defines distinct arithmetic classes. We establish that this arithmetic classification has direct physical consequences for long-wavelength charge fluctuations. In translation-covariant quasiperiodic systems, the infrared scaling of charge fluctuation is governed by the interplay between the irrationality exponent of irrational frequency and the large-harmonic decay of the hull charge profile: the latter determines the available charge weight, while the former controls how efficiently that weight is transferred to the infrared. Consequently, all algebraic irrational frequencies share the same arithmetic scaling, whereas exceptionally well-approximable transcendental frequencies can exhibit strongly enhanced infrared fluctuation scaling. We further prove that occupied states separated from the Fermi level by a gap stable throughout the hull contribute only an analytic infrared background, leaving the nontrivial scaling to near-Fermi states. Our results extend to general translation-covariant multi-frequency quasiperiodic systems.
