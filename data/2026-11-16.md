Abstract: Since Guerra's work, mean-field spin glasses have been studied through Hamilton--Jacobi equations. Here, using the Curie--Weiss and the Sherrington--Kirkpatrick models as prototypical examples, we show that his celebrated interpolation method is itself an optimal transport computation.
  After a linear change of variables the finite-$N$ pressure becomes an action $u$ with $\partial_t u = -A/2$ and $\partial_x u = -B$, where $B^2 \le A$, so $u$ is a classical subsolution of $\partial_t u+\frac12(\partial_x u)^2 \le 0$. Integrating along a straight segment gives Guerra's sum rule, and dropping its remainder makes $u(0,\cdot)$ and $u(t,\cdot)$ an admissible pair for the dual Kantorovich problem with quadratic cost. For the Curie--Weiss model and $t > 0$, $T_t(y) = y-t\tanh y$ is an optimal transport map from every source with finite first moment if and only if $t \le 1$, and the Kantorovich potentials are the limiting pressures. For the replica-symmetric Sherrington--Kirkpatrick model, the cavity map is injective on the physical half-line and the curvature of its Brenier potential is the longitudinal eigenvalue, which exceeds the replicon eigenvalue $Λ_{\mathrm{AT}}$: transport of Dirac masses does not detect the de Almeida--Thouless line. The replicon appears along the $\mathcal{W}_2$ geodesic that splits a Dirac mass into two atoms, as the second variation of a second-order surrogate of the two-atom Parisi functional. From a Dirac initial datum, Guerra's $K$-step bound takes Hopf--Lax form, and the Parisi formula becomes a Hopf--Lax formula over atomic measures. With a $\mathcal{W}_1$-Lipschitz bound for its initial datum, proved by comparing Cole--Hopf layers, we let $K\to\infty$ and recover at Dirac data the Hopf--Lax form of the Parisi formula due to Mourrat and to Mourrat and Panchenko, with a rate for equal-mass approximations.
