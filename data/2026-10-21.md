Motivation: Quantum batteries may outperform classical ones via collective quantum effects, but the precise conditions that enable a charging advantage (superextensive scaling) remain unclear. The authors aim to identify which structural features of the battery-charger interaction are necessary for scalable quantum advantage.

Method: The charging power is related to the norm of the commutator between battery and charger Hamiltonians, yielding an upper bound that captures only noncommuting terms. Working within a generic class of free-fermionic (quadratic) chargers, the authors analyze scaling with system size. They also revisit the participation number and define an effective version weighting the relative strength of contributing terms, then correlate it with charging-power scaling.

Result: Global operators prove insufficient for scalable superextensive charging; a cumulative hierarchy of many-body operators is necessary. The degree of advantage depends on battery structure. The effective participation number, accounting for relative term strengths, correlates with the observed charging-power scaling.

Conclusion: Cumulative locality is established as a key resource for quantum charging advantage, with results argued to extend beyond quadratic Hamiltonians.

Abstract: Quantum batteries can outperform their classical counterparts through collective quantum effects, yet the conditions enabling such advantage remain unclear. Using a generic class of free-fermionic chargers we prove that global operators are insufficient for scalable superextensive charging and that a cumulative hierarchy of many-body operators is required, with the structure of the battery determining the degree of advantage. This is obtained by relating the charging power to the norm of the commutator between the battery and charger Hamiltonians and deriving an upper bound only capturing noncommuting terms. Moreover, we revisit the concept of participation number and propose an effective definition that accounts for the relative strength of the contributing terms, correlating it with the charging power scaling. Not limited to quadratic Hamiltonians, our results establish cumulative locality as a key resource for quantum charging advantage.
