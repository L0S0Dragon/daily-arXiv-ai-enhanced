### [1] [Modeling Quantum Error Detection with Transition Matrices](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12073)
*Rohan S. Kumar,Ben Foxman,Yongshan Ding*

Main category: quant-ph

TL;DR: The paper introduces an explicit transition-matrix formalism for code-adapted basis-population dynamics of stabilizer codes under circuit-level stochastic Pauli noise, enabling analytical and interpretable analysis of multi-cycle quantum error detection protocols.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Analyzing quantum error detection (QED) protocols across multiple clock cycles is challenging because repeated syndrome extraction and post-selection couple logical-basis population dynamics with syndrome sectors. A tractable, exact, and interpretable description of these multi-cycle dynamics under realistic circuit-level noise is needed to study stationary behavior, leakage, and efficiency.

Method: The authors construct an explicit transition matrix in a code-adapted basis capturing probability flow between logical-basis labels and syndrome sectors for stabilizer codes under circuit-level stochastic Pauli noise. For QED, post-selection is incorporated by aggregating rejected outcomes into an absorbing state, so one matrix represents a full cycle of gates, syndrome extraction, and post-selection. They also derive conditions under which protocol symmetries allow exact lumping to simpler models, and evaluate the framework on the [[4,2,2]] and [[5,1,3]] codes under depolarizing gate noise and readout errors.

Result: Transition-matrix predictions agree with million-shot Stim Monte Carlo within reported confidence intervals. The framework identifies a leading-order obstruction to a stationary logical-only accepted-map description and quantifies accepted leakage injection from QED-check imperfections. It expresses leading-order QED efficiency as a function of check frequency in physically meaningful parameters and proves that less frequent checks improve efficiency to leading order. Matrix-computed results illustrate the first-cycle transient and interval-dependent efficiency.

Conclusion: The transition-matrix formalism provides both an analytical foundation for QED analysis and an interpretable tool for understanding code behavior under realistic noise, allowing classical stochastic-matrix techniques to be applied directly to multi-cycle QED protocols.

Abstract: We construct an explicit transition matrix that exactly describes code-adapted basis-population dynamics of stabilizer codes under circuit-level stochastic Pauli noise. The matrix is expressed in a code-adapted basis that captures probability flow between logical-basis labels and syndrome sectors. For quantum error detection (QED), we incorporate post-selection by aggregating rejected outcomes into an absorbing state, so that a single transition matrix represents a full clock cycle of gates, syndrome extraction, and post-selection. Additionally, we characterize when protocol symmetries permit exact lumping to simpler, more interpretable models.
  The transition matrix framework enables direct application of classical stochastic-matrix techniques to analyze multi-cycle QED protocols. First, we identify a leading-order obstruction to a stationary logical-only accepted-map description, and quantify the accepted leakage injection from QED-check imperfections that causes it. Second, we express leading-order QED efficiency as a function of check frequency in terms of physically meaningful parameters, and prove that less frequent checks improve efficiency to leading order. We evaluate the framework on the $[[4,2,2]]$ and $[[5,1,3]]$ codes under depolarizing gate noise and readout errors: transition-matrix predictions agree with million-shot Stim Monte Carlo at the scale of the reported confidence intervals, and matrix-computed results illustrate the first-cycle transient and interval-dependent efficiency. More broadly, the transition-matrix formalism provides both an analytical foundation for QED analysis and an interpretable tool for understanding code behavior under realistic noise.

### [2] [Fast quantum squeezing of a nanomechanical oscillator with an inverted potential](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12833)
*Oscar Schmitt Kremer,Lorenzo Dania,Lukas Novotny,Martin Frimmer*

Main category: quant-ph

TL;DR: The authors generate strong mechanical squeezing of a levitated silica nanoparticle's 800 kHz libration mode at room temperature by exposing the oscillator to an optically generated inverted potential, achieving a squeezed quadrature variance 11 dB below vacuum fluctuations within 250 ns.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Nonclassical mechanical states enable measurements beyond the standard quantum limit and are key resources for quantum sensing and metrology. However, the strongest squeezed mechanical states so far require cryogenic refrigeration and reservoir engineering on electromechanical platforms. Pushing further into the quantum-squeezed regime requires protocols that generate squeezing faster to overcome the decoherence rate at which state purity is lost.

Method: The 800 kHz libration mode of a silica nanoparticle optically levitated in vacuum at room temperature is squeezed. The mechanical oscillator is exposed to an optically generated inverted potential, under which the squeezing operation proceeds at an exponentially accelerated rate.

Result: A squeezed quadrature variance 11 dB below the vacuum fluctuations is reached within 250 ns.

Conclusion: The protocol establishes a new paradigm for generating quantum squeezing of mechanical motion and offers a platform for quantum-enhanced sensing with massive oscillators.

Abstract: Nonclassical states of nano- and micro-mechanical motion enable measurements beyond the standard quantum limit and constitute a key resource for quantum sensing and metrology. The most strongly squeezed mechanical states to date have been generated with electromechanical platforms under cryogenic refrigeration and using reservoir engineering. To push mechanical systems deeper into the quantum-squeezed regime requires protocols that increase the rate at which squeezing is generated to more strongly overcome the decoherence rate at which state purity is lost. Here, we squeeze the 800 kHz libration mode of a silica nanoparticle optically levitated in vacuum at room temperature. We expose our mechanical oscillator to an optically generated inverted potential, where the squeezing operation proceeds at an exponentially accelerated rate. We reach a squeezed quadrature variance 11 dB below the vacuum fluctuations within 250 ns. Our protocol establishes a new paradigm for generating quantum squeezing of mechanical motion and offers a platform for quantum-enhanced sensing with massive oscillators.

### [3] [State-adapted generalized mean-field projections for Pauli propagation of Heisenberg dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12840)
*Federico Tomás B. Pérez,Gabriela Wójtowicz,Martin B. Plenio*

Main category: quant-ph

TL;DR: The paper introduces a state-adapted Krylov framework that projects many-body observables onto low-body operator subspaces via geometric generalized mean-field projections, providing controlled-error, stable Heisenberg-picture simulation as an alternative to heuristic Pauli-string truncation.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Heisenberg-picture quantum many-body simulation is efficient only when evolved observables retain compact low-dimensional operator structure. Existing Pauli-string propagation and truncation methods exploit this but offer heuristic error control and poor stability under coherent Hamiltonian dynamics, necessitating a principled, stable framework.

Method: A state-adapted Krylov framework built on geometric generalized mean-field projections of many-body observables onto low-body operator subspaces. The dynamics approximate expectation values, respect Lieb-Robinson spatial support bounds, and compress high-body correlations onto state-relevant low-body representatives rather than discarding them. The authors derive necessary operator-entanglement obstructions to low-body representation and conditional sufficient bounds on representation and dynamical errors involving nonstabilizerness and controlled high-body tails.

Result: Numerical benchmarks demonstrate improved, stable finite-m hierarchies and simulations on large three-dimensional lattices.

Conclusion: The framework establishes a scalable state-adapted alternative to conventional Heisenberg-picture weight-truncation of Pauli strings, offering better error control and stability.

Abstract: The Heisenberg picture can make quantum many-body simulation efficient when evolved observables admit compact operator representation within low-dimensional structures. Conventional Pauli-string propagation and truncation techniques exploit this structure, but in coherent Hamiltonian dynamics their error control is often heuristic and their stability can be poor. We introduce a state-adapted Krylov framework based on geometric generalized mean-field projections of many-body observables onto low-body operator subspaces. The resulting dynamics approximate expectation values, do not extend spatial support beyond that prescribed by Lieb-Robinson bounds, and compress high-body correlations onto their state-relevant low-body representatives rather than simply discarding them. We derive necessary operator-entanglement obstructions to low-body representation and conditional sufficient bounds on representation and dynamical errors involving nonstabilizerness and controlled high-body tails. Numerical benchmarks show improved, stable finite-$m$ hierarchies and simulations on large three-dimensional lattices, establishing a scalable state-adapted alternative to conventional Heisenberg-picture weight-truncation of Pauli strings.

### [5] [Thermal robustness of sensing quantum phases of matter via qubit probes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12964)
*Sambunath Das,Hari Kumar Yadalam,Mark T. Mitchison,Katarzyna Roszak*

Main category: quant-ph

TL;DR: Temperature effects on a qubit probe's ability to distinguish phases in a spin-1/2 Heisenberg XXZ chain via pure dephasing are studied, showing phase-dependent decoherence persists at finite temperature.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Prior work showed that decoherence dynamics of a qubit probe coupled to a spin-1/2 Heisenberg XXZ chain differ between gapped and gapless phases at zero temperature, but it was unclear whether these signatures survive at finite temperature, which is essential for a robust and practical phase detector.

Method: A spin-1/2 Heisenberg XXZ chain is coupled to a qubit probe through a pure dephasing interaction. The decoherence dynamics of the probe are analyzed at zero and finite temperatures, with Luttinger liquid theory used to treat the gapless phase at low temperatures.

Result: In the gapped phase, strong oscillations appear and decay much more slowly than expected as temperature rises, with remnants still visible at infinite temperature. In the gapless phase at low temperatures, coherence shows asymptotic exponential decay in time, with a decay rate sensitive to the sign of the anisotropy parameter.

Conclusion: Probe dynamics continue to encode information about the chain even at finite, reasonably small temperatures, establishing qubit probes as sensitive and robust detectors for quantum phases of correlated spin systems.

Abstract: We study the effect of temperature on the ability of a qubit probe to distinguish between different phases in strongly correlated systems. Now to this end, we investigate a spin-1/2 Heisenberg XXZ chain coupled to a qubit probe via a pure dephasing interaction. At zero temperature, as reported previously, the decoherence dynamics exhibit different behavior in the gapped and gapless phases of the chain, with characteristic strong oscillations in the gapped phase and monotonic decay in the gapless phase. These oscillations decay much more slowly than expected with rising temperature in the gapped phase and their remnants are still visible at infinite temperature. In the gapless phase at low temperatures, using Luttinger liquid theory, we predict that the coherence exhibits asymptotic exponential decay in time, with the decay rate sensitive to the sign of the anisotropy parameter. We conclude that probe dynamics continue to carry information about the chain even at finite, reasonably small temperatures, positioning qubit probes as sensitive and robust detectors for quantum phases of correlated spin systems.

### [6] [QMA has perfect completeness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13032)
*Sabee Grewal,Dorian Rudolph*

Main category: quant-ph

TL;DR: The paper proves that QMA equals QMA_1, i.e., every QMA proof system can be converted into one with perfect completeness, using only Hadamard, Toffoli, and X gates, which form a universal gate set for QMA_1. Consequently, quantum 3-SAT is QMA-complete, and the construction relativizes to classical oracles, extending known QMA vs. QCMA oracle separations to QMA_1.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Understanding whether error-free (perfect completeness) verification is as powerful as bounded-error verification is a fundamental question in quantum complexity theory. Establishing QMA = QMA_1 addresses whether the completeness error can always be eliminated without loss of computational power, and determining universal gate sets for QMA_1 clarifies the physical resources needed.

Method: The authors give a constructive proof converting any quantum Merlin-Arthur proof system into one that is perfectly complete. The construction employs only Hadamard, Toffoli, and X gates, showing these constitute a universal gate set for QMA_1. They also analyze the relativization of the construction with respect to classical oracles.

Result: It is shown that QMA = QMA_1, so every QMA proof system can be made perfectly complete. The gate set {Hadamard, Toffoli, X} is universal for QMA_1. As a corollary, quantum 3-SAT is QMA-complete. Additionally, the construction relativizes to classical oracles, so existing classical-oracle separations of QMA from QCMA carry over to QMA_1.

Conclusion: Perfect completeness is achievable for QMA without reducing its power, giving QMA = QMA_1, with a simple universal gate set and implications for the QMA-completeness of quantum 3-SAT and for oracle separations between QMA_1 and QCMA.

Abstract: We prove $\mathsf{QMA} = \mathsf{QMA_1}$, i.e., every quantum Merlin-Arthur proof system can be made perfectly complete. Our construction uses only Hadamard, Toffoli, and $X$ gates, yielding a universal gate set for $\mathsf{QMA_1}$. As a consequence, quantum $3$-SAT is $\mathsf{QMA}$-complete. The construction relativizes to classical oracles, so known classical-oracle separations of $\mathsf{QMA}$ from $\mathsf{QCMA}$ extend to $\mathsf{QMA_1}$.

### [7] [On non-phononic modes and glassy dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12665)
*Felix C. Mocanu,Dmytro Khomenko,Matteo Paoluzzi,Giancarlo Ruocco,Walter Schirmacher,Grzegorz Szamel,Francesco Zamponi*

Main category: cond-mat.dis-nn

TL;DR: Glassy model potentials with identical molecular and swap dynamics but different truncated interactions show that low-frequency non-phononic vibrational modes can differ dramatically at fixed structure and dynamics, challenging the link between these modes and supercooled relaxation.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The connection between vibrational modes and glassy dynamics has been debated for decades, and it remains unclear whether low-frequency non-phononic vibrational modes control or merely correlate with slow supercooled liquid relaxation.

Method: A family of glass-forming model potentials is introduced that share identical molecular and swap dynamics but differ in their low-frequency vibrational modes. Small systems are used so that low-frequency modes do not hybridize with phonons, allowing their statistical properties to be studied separately. The study examines the roles of the energy-minimization algorithm, finite-size effects, interaction-potential truncation, and parent temperature.

Result: Models with identical supercooled liquid structure and dynamics are found to possess dramatically different low-frequency non-phononic vibrational modes depending on how the interaction potential is truncated.

Conclusion: These findings challenge the idea that low-frequency non-phononic vibrational modes are responsible for the slow relaxation of supercooled liquids.

Abstract: The relationship between vibrational modes and glassy dynamics has been an important and controversial topic of study for many decades. We introduce a family of glass-forming model potentials that have identical molecular and swap dynamics but different low-frequency vibrational modes. In sufficiently small systems low frequency modes do not hybridize with phonons, and this enables us to study their statistical properties independently. We investigate i) the role of the optimization algorithm used to find a minimum; ii) that of the finite-size effects; iii) that of the truncation of the interaction potential; and, iv) that of the parent temperature. We find that models with identical supercooled liquid structure and dynamics can have dramatically different non-phononic vibrational modes at low frequency, based on how the potential is truncated. These findings challenge ideas relating the low-frequency non-phononic vibrational modes with the slow supercooled liquid relaxation.

</details>
